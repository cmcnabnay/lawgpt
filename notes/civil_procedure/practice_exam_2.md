Rule
Rule 4a of the Federal Rule of Civil Procedure holds that a summons must serve a summons with the copy of the complaint within 90 days of initially filing said complaint. Rule 4(c) mandates that the server must be an individual who is at least 18 years old and not a party. On the other side of this interaction, Rule 4(e) governs, it holds that the individual being served must not be a minor. Rule 4(e) further stipulates the methods of service which include delivering a copy of the summons and the complain in person, leaving each component at the individual's dwelling with someond of suitable age and discretion is present, and delivering a copy of each component to an agent to receive service of process. 

Analysis 
In order to evaluate the constitutonality of the notices served to each defndant, one must consider Rule 4 of the Federal Rule of Civil Procedure. Regarding Rule 4(a),it was stated in the explanation that the summons contained all the required information so it assumed that Rule 4(a) is fulfilled. Pursuant to rule 4(c), the server was Lola who is presumably over the age of 18, as she is a lawyer, furthermore, she is not a party in this case, she merely represents a party. Thus the services are premissible according to rule 4(c). In the case of Anna, the plaintiff took the necessary steps to ensure that the defendant would receive notice of her impending legal affairs, however, since Masie played a ruse on her sister, this complicates the proceedings. The plaintiff fulfilled her obligations, and Lola could not have reasonably predicted she would have been fooled. It is the responsibility of the signator to ensure that their signature is legible.
