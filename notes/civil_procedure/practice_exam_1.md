### Question 1

Pat is filing a complaint at his private law school that they are undermining his first amendment rights. The defense wants to argue that the first amendment should not apply because it is a private institutions. What motion should the defense use?

- **A.** 12(b)(1): Lack of subject matter jurisdiction
- **B.** 12(b)(4): Insufficient process
- **C.** 12(b)(6): Failure to state a claim upon which relief can be granted
- **D.** 12(c): Judgment on the pleadings, made after the pleadings are closed
- **E.** 12(e): For a pleading that is so vague that a party cannot respond

> **Answer: C. 12(b)(6)**, the defense is essentially saying that the right to relief does not apply here because the action that would warrant the relief does not apply to a private institution

- **B.** Plaintiff will not be able to discover a copy of the report, but Plaintiff will be able to ask the insurer in a deposition if Waterslide Co. made the slide

## Part 2: Essay

### Introduction

This case addresses a dispute between a marine biologist and the United States Navy. The Navy is testing a Sound Navigation and Ranging (SONAR) system. The marine biologist seeks an injunction on the training exercises on the grounds that the technology poses a risk to marine mammals. During the discovery phase of the trial, the biologist requested documents that detailed the workings of the sonar system. Realizing that the open sourcing of such documents would pose a national security risk, the Navy, in response, filed a motion for a protetive order that was denied by the District Court. We thus find ourselves in a paradigm where the Navy has appealed this decision.

### Rule

The impetus for this case is the act passed by the Congress allowing a party to seek an injunction against the United States Navy if they show they have a professional interest in marine life and that Navy exercises are likely to harm marine life. The dispute this memo addresses centers around whether the Navy must disclose information requested by the plaintiff in a deposition. Rule 26(c) of the Rules of Federal Civil Procedure grants the right of a party to move for a protective order in the court where the action is pending. This rule requires a certification that the movant has in good faith conferred or attempted to confer with other affected party in an effort to resolve the dispute without court action. Furthermore, the court can issue an order to protect a party from annoyance, embarassment, oppression, or undue burden or expense by requiring that a deposition be sealed and opened only on court order.

### Analysis

The plaintiff's pursuit of an injunction comes into question. The first requirement of the Congress' law is satisfied because the plaintiff is a marine biologist, thus, it is evident that plaintiff has a professional interst in marine life. However the plaintiff merely alleges that sonar training will harm marine mammals and has not show this yet. Prudence indeed will dictate that no military secrets shall be revealed for light and transient causes. From the perspective of the Navy, making public sensitive information about defense technology over a dispute that it was potentially harming marine mammals would be a light and transient cause.

In order to satisfy the requirements for Rule 26(c), the Navy must show that it had made good faith attempts with the marine biologist to resolve the dispute in court. The Navy has made it clear that they are willing to produce the sensitive documents for the purposes of the trial. The question then becomes, does this expression constitute an effort to confer with the marine biologist without a court action? It not because tha Navy has offered a compromise to resolve the dispute, the plaintiff receives the documents for the purpose of the trial, while documents will retain a classified status. This is a proposal that is acceptable to the plantiff because their objective pertaining to the documents is not publicity but merely possession so that they can extract the relevant information from them in order to inform their prosecution strategy. Therefore, the requirement that an attempt to confer with the other affected party must be present is satisfied. The rule can thus be applied which would require the sensitive documents to be sealed and opened only on court order.

### Conclusion

In light of the prior analysis, a court would likely grant the United States Navy's request for a motion to seal certain sensitive documents. The reasoning holds that Rule 26(c) bestows upon a party the right to move for a protective order if that party has made an attempt to settle a dispute over disclosures without a court order. The Navy's expression of willingness to transfer the documents constitutes an attempt to confer with the other party. As a result the court will most likely grant the protective order.
