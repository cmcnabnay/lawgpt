Question 1

The issue before the court concerns whether it has subject matter jurisdiction over Tatiana's conversion and trespass claims against Downing Lumber.

Federal courts are courts of limited jurisdiction, there are two bases of jurisdiction that are relevant in this cases, federal question jurisdiction and diversity jurisdiction. A claim arises under federal law when federal law creates the cause of action. A state law claim can also arise under federal law if the federal issue is (1) necessarily raised, (2) actually disputed, (3) substantial, and (4) capable of resolution in federal court without disrupting the federal-state balance. The federal issue must arise from the plaintiff’s claim, an anticipated defense on federal grounds is insufficient for federal question jurisdiction.

Diversity jurisdiction requires that the parties in a suit do not share citizenship of the same state with any opposing party and that the amount in controversy exceeds $75,000. An individual is a citizen of the state where they are domiciled, meaning the state where they live and intend to remain indefinitely. Domicile is presumed to continue until it changes, and it is measured on the date the complaint is filed. A corporation is a citizen of its state of incorporation and its principal place of business, which is the place where its officers direct, control, and coordinate its activities. Tatiana’s claims are conversion and trespass, and the tax sales only comes up because Downing raised it in a defense. Under the well-pleaded complaint rule, federal jurisdiction does not exist when the federal question appears in the defense.

A. Federal Question Jurisdiction

(1) Considering the first element of the Grable test for state law claims raising federal issues, the federal issue is likely not necessarily raised. The only federal issue in this case is whether the IRS validly sold 100 acres of Tatiana’s orchard to Downing to cover her back taxes. This issue only enters the case through Downing’s response to Tatiana’s claim. Tatiana’s complaint only brings up conversion and trespass which are state law claims, she does not need to reference the tax sale to bring either claim. Tatiana will likely argue that the federal issue is necessarily raised because she must prove that she holds the title to the land and her title depends upon whether the tax sale was valid.

(2) For the second element, the issue is actually disputed. Downing claims that it bought the 100 acres in question from the IRS and Tatiana was “shocked” to learn about the tax sale and she maintains that she continues to own the land.

(3) For the third element, the issue is substantial because the ability for the IRS to sell property to collect back taxes and give buyers a title matters to the federal system of law beyond the two parties in this case.

(4) For the fourth element, it is not clear that the court could hear this case without disrupting the division of work between federal and state courts. Disputes over title to land, conversion, and trespass are usually decided by state courts under state law. If every conversion or trespass claim where the defendant points to a federal sale could be heard in federal court, a large number of ordinary property cases would move into federal court.

B. Diversity Jurisdiction

Downing is incorporated in Minnesota, so it is a Minnesota citizen. Downing will likely argue that it is also a Michigan citizen because its official headquarters is the lodge in Western Michigan, where its leadership meets. If Downing were to succeed in this claim, diversity jurisdiction would not be present and the case would be removed from federal court. However, Downing is likely not a Michigan citizen. The lodge is not where Downing conducts it control over the company, it is a retreat used once a year and a vacation location for the rest of the year. The company’s nerve center is likely at the company’s offices at their biggest lumberyard in southern Minnesota because this is where the President and two of the Vice Presidents work from. In Hertz, the court specifically states that the location of an annual retreat is not the nerve center of a company.

Tatiana was likely domiciled in Michigan when she filed this suit on November 1, 2024. It is evident that on November 13, 2024, Tatiana became a citizen of Texas because she physically relocated to Texas and publicly announced that she intended to leave her previous domicile of Michigan and never return. Her voter registration, driver’s license, and address changes occurred after the suit was filed so they do not count toward establishing her as a Texas citizen. Tatiana started as a Michigan citizen because she grew up in that state and returned after college. Michigan remains her domicile unless she both lived in Texas and intended to remain there indefinitely. It is not evident that both of these events happened prior to November 1. She did live in Texas every year from November to February, beginning in 2020, but this was for only three to four months out of the year. Intent is less evident, there was evidence pointing towards Michigan citizenship, she still lived in Michigan for most of the year, voted in there, had a car registered in both states. Meanwhile other evidence pointed toward intent to live in Texas such as telling people that her heart was really in Texas and planning to retire in Texas. The facts on November 1, 2024 point more strongly towards Tatiana being a citizen of Michigan with a clear switch of citizenship only becoming evident on November 13, after the suit was filed.

The parties are likely completely diverse. Tatiana was most likely a citizen of Michigan at the time of filing and Downing was most likely a citizen of Minnesota. Even if the court found Tatiana was a citizen of Texas before her permanent move to the state, she would still be a citizen of a different state than Downing, and thus diversity jurisdiction would be met.

The amount in controversy requirement is met. Tatiana seeks $200,000 for conversion and $70,000 for trespass. The two claims together total $270,000 which exceeds the $75,000 threshold, therefore the amount in controversy requirement is satisfied for diversity jurisdiction.



Question 2

The issue before us concerns whether Rooney, Wade, Salazar, and Downing Lumber would each be subject to personal jurisdiction in Iowa state court for Haas's negligence claim.

Since the Iowa long arm statute reaches as far as the Constitution allows, the only question is whether jurisdiction over each defendant satisfies the Due Process Clause of the Fourteenth Amendment. A defendant is subject to general jurisdiction on any claim in a state where he is at home, which for an individual is their domicile and for a corporation is its state of incorporation and principal place of business. An individual that is voluntarily present in the state is also subject to general jurisdiction there and a corporation may consent to jurisdiction by registering to do business in a state whose law treats registration as consent. Otherwise, a defendant is subject to specific jurisdiction if (1) the defendant has minimum contacts with the forum state that he created, (2) the claim arises out of or relates to those contacts, and (3) exercising jurisdiction is fair and reasonable.

A. General Jurisdiction and Service

In this case, non of the defendants are likely subject to general jurisdiction in Iowa. Rooney, Wade, and Salazar all live in Minnesota, so Minnesota is their domicile and the only state where they are at home. Downing is incorporated in Minnesota and its principal place of business is its lumberyard in southern Minnesota. Thus, Downing is also only at home in Minnesota. Haas could argue that Downing ships lumber throughout the Midwest, and Iowa may be one of the states that it ships to, thus Downing is at home in Iowa as well. This would be a weak argument because the evidence clearly shows the company is domiciled in Minnesota. Under Daimle, a corporation is not at home in every state where it does a substantial amount of business. The facts do not suggest that any of the individual defendants will be served while in Iowa. Rooney was in Iowa on the day of the accident, however, he has presumably since left the state, thus, Haas would have to serve him while he is voluntarily present in Iowa. Downing could be subject to general jurisdiction if it has registered to do business in Iowa or one of the individual defendants is served there,.

B. Rooney

It is evident that Rooney has purposeful contacts with Iowa. He physically drove a truck into Iowa, which was his own choice. Wade told him to go through Wisconsin, but Rooney decided to cut across Iowa in order to save gas money. He entered Iowa before Wade ever threatened to fire him, thus his entrance into Iowa was voluntary. Haas's claim arises directly out of Rooney’s contact with Iowa, since the logs fell off the truck and hit Haas's car on Route 35 in Iowa. Specific jurisdiction here is likely fair because the burden on Rooney is small, since he lives in Minnesota, which borders Iowa. Furthermore, Iowa has a strong interest in accidents on its highways that injure its residents. Therefore, Rooney is likely subject to personal jurisdiction in Iowa.

C. Wade

Wade’s case is less clear because he never entered Iowa. Wade will likely argue that the truck was only in Iowa because of Rooney's unilateral decision to disobey his directions. However, Wade’s phone call with Rooney likely created a contact with Iowa. Rooney told Wade that he was in Iowa and was worried about the logs, and suggested to stop in Iowa for help or drive five miles back to the lumberyard. Wade refused both options then ordered Rooney to continue through Iowa to Illinois and threatened to fire him if he did not follow the directions. Wade deliberately directed his employee to drive a truck he knew might be unstable through Iowa. This could be seen as Wade entering into Iowa through an agent which is relevant contact with the forum state. Haas's claim relates to that contact, because the truck spilled its logs on Route 35 which was the exact route Wade ordered. Jurisdiction over Wade is likely fair, Wade lives near the lumberyard, a few miles from the Iowa border, and Iowa has a strong interest in highway safety. Thus, Wade is likely subject to personal jurisdiction in Iowa.

D. Salazar

Salazar likely has no meaningful contacts with Iowa. Salazar loaded the logs onto the truck at the lumberyard in Minnesota and had no other role. Salazar could not have known that the truck would go through Iowa because the planned route went through Wisconsin. The truck only reached Iowa because of Rooney's decision and Wade's later order, which are acts that Salazar did not have control over. Salazar’s role here is similar to that of the dealer in World Wide Volkswagen. The dealer did not have meaningful contact with the state of Oklahoma, it was the customers who purchased a car from them that drove the vehicle into that state. The dealer had no control over this act. Because Salazar did not have purposeful contacts with the state of Iowa, he is likely not subject to personal jurisdiction in Iowa.

E. Downing

Downing likely has purposeful contacts with Iowa through its employees. A corporation can only act through its agents as International Shoe shows because the company's contacts with Washington came from its salesmen working there. Rooney was an agent of Downing. Downing could argue that Rooney's detour broke the company’s instructions. However, once Wade, another agent of Downing, learned that the truck was in Iowa and ordered it to continue across the state, Downing could be said to have established contacts with Iowa. Haas's claim arises out of these contacts, because he was injured by the Downing shipment on the Iowa highway that Downing's employees chose. Jurisdiction is fair, since Downing's main lumberyard is only a few miles from Iowa and Iowa has a strong interest in the safety of its residents. Therefore, Downing is likely subject to personal jurisdiction in Iowa.

Rooney chose to drive into Iowa and caused the accident there. Wade ordered the unstable truck to continue along specific Iowa highways after Rooney warned him about the potential danger. Downing acted in Iowa through Rooney and Wade in order to complete its sale. Salazar only loaded the truck in Minnesota and had no part in sending it to Iowa. Rooney and Wade are likely subject to personal jurisdiction in Iowa, while Salazar is not. All defendants are subject to general jurisdiction in Minnesota because they are all at home in that state. Therefore, Rooney, Wade, and Downing can be sued in Iowa, but not Salazar.
