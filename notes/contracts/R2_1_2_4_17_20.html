## **Contracts Reading Notes**

### **1. RULES**

**Rule R2 §1: Contract Defined**
- **Meaning:** A contract is a promise or a set of promises enforceable by law.
- **Legal Question:** What constitutes a legally binding agreement?
- **Requirements:**
1. A promise or set of promises.
2. Breach of the promises must provide a remedy.

**Result if satisfied:** Legally binding contract exists.

---

**Rule R2 §2: Promise; Promisor; Promisee; Beneficiary**
- **Meaning:** Defines key terms related to promises and obligations in contracts.
- **Legal Question:** Who are the parties involved in a promise?
- **Requirements:**
1. Promisor (the one making the promise).
2. Promisee (the one receiving the promise).
3. Beneficiary (a third party who may benefit from the promise).

**Result if satisfied:** Clear understanding of roles in contractual obligations.

---

**Rule R2 §4: How a Promise May Be Made**
- **Meaning:** Promises can be made verbally, in writing, or inferred from conduct.
- **Legal Question:** How can a promise be communicated?

---

**Rule R2 §17(1): Requirement of a Bargain**
- **Meaning:** A contract requires mutual assent and consideration.
- **Legal Question:** How is mutual assent achieved?
- **Requirements:**
1. Manifestation of mutual assent.
2. Presence of consideration (something of value exchanged).

**Result if satisfied:** Binding contract formed.

---

**Rule R2 §20: Effect of Misunderstanding**
- **Meaning:** No contracts are formed if parties attach materially different meanings to their manifestations with no mutual awareness.
- **Legal Question:** When does misunderstanding negate mutual assent?
- **Requirements:**
1. Materially different meanings.
2. Lack of awareness of the differing interpretations.

**Result if satisfied:** No contract is enforced.

---

**Rule R2 §153: When Mistake of One Party Makes a Contract Voidable**
- **Meaning:** A contract is voidable by the mistaken party if the mistake significantly impacts the agreement.
- **Legal Question:** When can a mistaken party void a contract?
- **Requirements:**
1. Basic assumption mistake.
2. Material effect on agreed exchange.
3. Other party knew or should have known of the mistake, or enforcement would be unconscionable.

**Result if satisfied:** Contract can be voided.

### **2. IMPORTANT DISTINCTIONS**

- **Offer vs. Invitation to Make an Offer:** An offer creates a power of acceptance; an invitation does not.

- **Revocation vs. Rejection:** Revocation is the offeror withdrawing an offer; rejection is the offeree declining an offer.

- **Acceptance vs. Counteroffer:** Acceptance agrees to the original terms; a counteroffer alters those terms, thus rejecting the original offer.

- **Promise vs. Performance:** A promise is a commitment; performance is the act of fulfilling that promise.

- **Bilateral vs. Unilateral Contract:** A bilateral contract involves mutual promises; a unilateral contract involves a promise exchanged for performance.

### **5. IMPORTANT TERMS**

**Term:** Contract
- **Meaning:** Legally enforceable promise.
- **Why it matters:** Contracts create binding obligations that can be enforced by law.

**Term:** Unilateral Contract
- **Meaning:** A contract where one party's promise is exchanged for performance.
- **Why it matters:** Provides a framework for agreements where one party acts and binds the other.

### **6. HOW THE RULES FIT TOGETHER**

R2 §§1, 2, 4, and 17 together define foundational aspects of contracts, including what constitutes a valid offer, the roles of parties in a contract, and the necessity of mutual assent and consideration. R2 §20 addresses issues of misunderstanding that can preclude contract formation, while §153 deals with unilateral mistakes affecting the validity of a contract. These rules collectively create a comprehensive structure for understanding how contracts are formed and enforced.
