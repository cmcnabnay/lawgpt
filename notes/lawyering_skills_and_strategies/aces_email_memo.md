Mr. Gurvin will likely not win his copyright infringement claim because while his design is substantially similar to the released logo by the Aces, the alleged infringer, a designer for ForwardDesigns could not have been reasonably expected to have access to Gurvin's logo. 

"It is rare that direct evidence of copyright infrincement exists. Therefore, the courts have turned to circumstantial evidence in determining whether one work infrinces upon another." Sativa v. Malcom (D. Frank 2003). Thus courts in this circuit have applied a two prong test for infringement 1) Are the works "substantially similar"? and 2) Did the alleged infringer have access to the copyrighted work? In Fred Fisher, an artist accussed of plagirism of a bass line was found liable for infringement because he was a working songwriter who kept up with popular current music and therefore must have heard it and had access to it. Fred Fisher, Inc. v. Dillingham, 298 F. 145 (S.D.N.Y 1924). In Bright Tunes Music Corp., George Harrison of the Beatles was found liable for infringement because he had "unconsciously" copied the song in contest, even though the court believed him that he did not consciously copy the earlier song. Bright Tunes Music Corp. v. Harrisongs
Music, Ltd., 420 F. Supp. 177 (S.D.N.Y. 1976), aff’d sub nom ABKCO Music Inc. v.
Harrisongs Music, Ltd., 722 F.2d 988 (2d Cir. 1983)

In Savia, it was determined that Malcom was not liable for infrincement as a result, she was granted summary judgment. The court found that the works were virtually identical, however, they concluded that Malcom could not have accessed Savia's song because it was released to the public only in the form of closing credits of a motion picture, that motion picture only had a limited run in Franklin City, and at the time of the showing, Malcom was far below the age restriction required to see the film. Even if the age limit was violated, it would be still be implausible for a four year old to be admitted to see a NC-17 rated movie. Savia's argument rested upon speculation, claiming that Malcom might have heard the song "Perhaps" through someone who had seen the motion picture play the song. However, the court dismissed this argument because there was no evidence to justify that conclusion. 

The circustances of Savia are similar to that of Gurvin. In Savia, the songs were "virtually identical". In Gurvin, the logos in controversy are virtually identical, both depict a hand holding four ace carts that rotate color in the same order, both ending with an ace of spades. Thus, the first prong for infringement that courts in this circuit use, substantial similarity, is satisfied. In Savia, the court concluded that there was no plausible evidence that Malcom had access to Savia's work and as a result, prong two of the infringement analysis could not be satisfied. It it likely that Monica Dean similarly did not have access to Gurvin's logo. Gurvin's design was submitted to Franklin Sports Authority in September 2025, however, the submission was discarded shortly thereafter. Daniel Luce did not pass the design to ForwardDesigns, what's more, Lcuce did not have contact with anyone from ForwardDesigns. Dean drew the logo in about February 2026 and submitted it to ProBall along with a dozen other designs, they are the entity that selected the final logo design. No one from Franklin Sports Authority had contact with representatives from ProBall, thus it is unlikely that Gurvin's design made it to ProBall, who eventually selected the logo design. Unlike in Fred Fisher, Inc., where the court held that an artist infringed because the song was publicly available and an artist in the music industry would have been reasonably expected to have heard the song prior, Dean could not be expected to have seen Gurvin's logo before, subconsciously influencing the design, because it was not publicly available. The only means through which Dean could have gained access to Gurvin's logo was through SportsBall by way of Franklin Sports Authority. The evidence does not support any connection between Franklin Sports Authoity and SportsBall or between Franklin Sports Authority and ForwardDesigns. 

It is evident that Gurvin's logo and the logo designed by Monica Dean which was selected to be the official logo of the Franklin City Aces are substantially similar. However, the evidence shows that Dean could not have had access to Gurvin's logo because it was not publicly available nor was it distributed to anyone in her company ForwardDesigns. Thus, the alleged infringement does not satisfy both prongs of copyright infringement used by courts of this circuit. Therefore, Gurvin's claim of copyright infringement is unlikely to succeed.
