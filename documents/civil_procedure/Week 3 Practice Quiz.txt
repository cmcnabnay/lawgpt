In the State of O'Quinn, a defendant has committed the tort of "conversion" if (a) the plaintiff has rightful possession of a piece of property, (b) the defendant intentionally takes that property, and (c) the defendant did not have permission to take the property.

P sues D for conversion. Assume that the case is in federal court. And assume that the following is the relevant text of P's Complaint:

P had a car, which P bought last year.
D drove off with P's car.
While driving off, D shouted, "Haha, I took your car!"
D did not ask for, nor did P give, P's permission to take the car.
P therefore requests $100,000 in damages for the fair market value of the car.

D files a motion to dismiss, arguing that the Complaint has failed to cite the O'Quinn state statutes and caselaw establishing the tort of conversion. You are the judge in the case: Is D right that the Complaint should be dismissed on this basis?

(Remember, although it's state tort law, we're in federal court for this hypo, so the federal standard we learned applies. We'll learn when a state-law case can be in federal court later this semester.)

Group of answer choices
Yes, D is right and the Complaint should be dismissed on this basis.
No, D is wrong and the Complaint should not be dismissed on this basis.
