P and D had a contract, in which P agreed to pay $5,000 for D’s used car. On August 1, 2022, P handed over the $5,000, and D handed over the car. “It runs great," D said, "enjoy!” But that afternoon, when P went to drive the car for the first time, it wouldn’t start. P’s neighbor, a mechanic, looked at it and said: “That engine’s totally ruined.” The next week, on August 7, P went to D’s house and asked for his money back. D refused. P was mad.

Six months later, P happened to see D at a restaurant. P complained again about his car. Things got heated, and D punched P in the jaw. P didn't do anything more right away, but he stayed mad for months.

Eventually, P talked to a lawyer. On August 20, 2024, P’s lawyer filed a lawsuit with two claims: (1) breach of contract for the faulty car sale and (2) a tort claim for D hitting P in the jaw. The relevant state law has a two-year statute of limitations for both contract claims and tort claims. If D argues that P's lawsuit is barred by the statute of limitations, what should the court do?

Group of answer choices
Allow both claims to proceed.
Allow the contract claim to proceed, but dismiss the tort claim as time-barred.
Dismiss the contract claim as time-barred, but allow the tort claim to proceed.
Dismiss both claims as time-barred.

Question 6
1 pts

P owns a house at the front of a 10-acre lot. D moved into a house on a small lot at the back of P's lot. D is 92 years old and uses an electric wheelchair. Therefore, D needs a ramp to get into D's new house. But the house did not come with a ramp. So the day that D moved in, D had workers install a ramp to the front door. It was the only place on the house where a ramp could easily be installed. P noticed the ramp and believed that the end of it extended about two feet onto P's property. So P sued D. And P asked the court for a preliminary injunction requiring D to remove the ramp during the litigation.

The judge held a preliminary injunction hearing, at which the following evidence emerged. D testified that she believes the ramp is entirely on her property. D also testified that she would not be able to enter her house without the ramp, and that if a different ramp needed to be installed, she would have to spend thousands of dollars to cut a new door into the other side of her house, which would take at least a month. D testified that the current metal ramp can be easily packed up and removed, because it is not permanently embedded in the ground. In turn, P testified that he believes the ramp extends a few feet onto his property. P also testified that it is at the back of his property, and he couldn't remember the last time he had been in that corner of his lot before D moved in (someone else cut P's grass).

You are a legal research assistant for the judge (called a clerk). The judge tells you that she thinks each side has an equal chance of winning the case in the end, and the case would certainly take at least a few months, because expert surveyors would have to survey the land. The judge then asks you whether she should grant the preliminary injunction requiring D to remove the ramp. Assuming that the court uses the exact same rules as a federal court, what should you answer?

Group of answer choices
(A) Grant the preliminary injunction, because P has shown an irreparable injury.
(B) Deny the preliminary injunction, because P has not shown an irreparable injury, but D would be irreparably injured.
(C) Deny the preliminary injunction, because the balance of the equities likely tips in favor of D.
(D) Both B and C. Which is to say: Deny the preliminary injunction, because P would not be irreparably harmed, D would be irreparably injured, and the balance of the equities likely tips in favor of D.
